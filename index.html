01

Overview

I came into machine learning through a circuit-theory question, and kept the circuits.

I'm a fourth-year Electrical & Electronic Engineering undergraduate at Daffodil International University. Most of what I build spans the whole path from schematic to PCB to firmware to data to model — because the interesting constraints only show up when you own all of it.

My current focus is energy-efficient inference at the edge: spiking and recurrent networks small enough to run on an ESP32 or STM32, applied to solar power forecasting and always-on audio. I also work on Bangla-language NLP for health applications.

What I actually enjoy is the debugging — the SD card that only fails on the 5 V rail, the breadboard contact resistance that quietly costs you three days of data. Hardware teaches you fast that a design isn't finished until it has survived a week outdoors.

SpikeSoil

Solar PV monitoring & forecasting edge node

An ESP32-S3 data-acquisition node — dual INA219 current/voltage sensors, BH1750 irradiance, DS18B20 panel temperature — logging to SD card every five seconds with sync-per-write and retry logic. Rooftop-validated on 3 W panels under resistive load. Paired with forecasters under 5,000 parameters, small enough to run on the same microcontroller that collects the data.

DengueShield BD

Bangla-language clinical triage + outbreak forecasting

A two-model system for dengue response in Bangladesh: a fine-tuned BanglaBERT classifier that triages symptom descriptions written in Bangla, and an XGBoost model forecasting next-7-day case counts across nine divisions. Prepared for demonstration at IEEE ICADHI 2026.

85.0% accuracy on a hand-built adversarial test set (severe-class recall 0.80); outbreak MAE 5.36 on 5,181 DGHS records validated with a chronological hold-out split. The synthetic split scored near 100% — a memorisation artifact, which is why it isn't the headline number.

Keyword spotting on STM32 — ANN vs. SNN

Research · write-up in progress

A matched-parameter benchmark on Google Speech Commands: roughly 23,000 parameters each, 92.0% for the dense network against 86.0% for the spiking one at a 22% spike rate. The point is to quantify exactly what accuracy you trade for sparsity inside the 128 KB RAM budget of an STM32F411.
